The Name

۞

This Surah is named Al-Fatihah because of its subject-matter. Fatihah is opening, whether of a book, a door, or a matter. Other names include Al-Hamd (The Praise), As-Salah (The Prayer), and Ash-Shifa (The Healing).

Group 1 · The Request for Guidance

۞
1

بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ

In the name of Allah, the Most Merciful, the Most Compassionate.

Before beginning anything, one should adopt the principle of reciting the Basmalah. One of the most important benefits is that when one begins something by reciting Allah's name, the act is brought under His protection and is granted blessing.

2

الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ

All praise and thanks belong to Allah, the Lord of the entire universe.

The nature of this surah is that of a du'a — one that begins with praise, moves through declaration, and ends in request. Praise first, then asking: the order itself is the teaching.

Al-Hamd means all praise and all thanks belong to Allah exclusively — recognised by the tongue, the heart, and the limbs.
3

الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ

The Most Merciful, the Most Compassionate.

The root of Ar-Rahman and Ar-Rahim is ra-ha-meem, which literally means the 'womb' — carrying the sense of nurturing tenderness that envelops and sustains.

Note

His mercy precedes His judgement: the attributes of mercy are named before the Master of the Day of Judgement is introduced.
Ask the tafseer
Why does Al-Fatihah open with praise before making a request?

The surah is structured as a du'a, and the order is deliberate: it establishes the correct posture before asking. Praise and gratitude come first, then the declaration of worship, and only then the request for guidance — so the ask emerges from recognition rather than demand.
