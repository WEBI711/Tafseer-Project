The Request for Guidance
1:1Basmalah

بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ

In the name of Allah, the Most Merciful, the Most Compassionate.

Before beginning anything, one adopts the principle of reciting the Basmalah. It places the act under His protection and opens it to blessing — a declaration of dependence rather than a formality.

1:2Praise

الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ

All praise and thanks belong to Allah, Lord of the entire universe.

The surah is a du'a, and it opens with praise before it makes any request. Praise first, then asking: the order is itself the teaching.

Root study

ح-م-د — praise joined with gratitude, expressed by the tongue, the heart, and the limbs. Rabb adds the sense of one who creates, sustains, and brings a thing to completion.

1:3Mercy

الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ

The Most Merciful, the Most Compassionate.

The root ra-ha-meem literally means the 'womb' — carrying the sense of a nurturing tenderness that envelops and sustains what it holds.

Why does Al-Fatihah open with praise before making a request?

The surah is structured as a du'a, and the order is deliberate: it establishes the correct posture before asking. Praise and gratitude come first, then the declaration of worship, and only then the request for guidance — so the ask emerges from recognition rather than demand.
