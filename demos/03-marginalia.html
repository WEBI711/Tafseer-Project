The Opening

سورة الفاتحة

Seven ayat, recited in every unit of prayer — a supplication Allah Himself taught, answering the question the Qur'an was revealed to address.

Makki7 ayatGuidanceJuz 1
1:1

بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ

In the name of Allah, the Most Merciful, the Most Compassionate.

Every act of consequence is opened with His name. The Basmalah is not a formality but a declaration of dependence — it places the act under His protection and invites blessing into it.

Also named Al-Hamd · As-Salah · Ash-Shifa · Umm al-Kitab
1:2

الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ

All praise and thanks belong to Allah, Lord of the entire universe.

The surah is a du'a, and it begins with praise before it makes any request. This order teaches something: gratitude is the correct posture from which to ask.

Rabb is not merely 'lord' — it carries the sense of one who creates, sustains, and progressively brings a thing to completion.

Root ح-م-د — praise joined with gratitude, of the tongue, the heart, and the limbs
۞
1:3—1:4

الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ ۝ مَالِكِ يَوْمِ الدِّينِ

The Most Merciful, the Most Compassionate. Master of the Day of Judgement.

Mercy is named twice before mastery is introduced once. The One who will hold the account is presented first as the One who nurtures — the sequence is deliberate.

Why does Al-Fatihah open with praise before making a request?

The surah is structured as a du'a, and the order is deliberate: it establishes the correct posture before asking. Praise and gratitude come first, then the declaration of worship, and only then the request for guidance — so the ask emerges from recognition rather than demand.
