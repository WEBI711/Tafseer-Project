2:180 · Bequests0.94

كُتِبَ عَلَيْكُمْ إِذَا حَضَرَ أَحَدَكُمُ الْمَوْتُ إِن تَرَكَ خَيْرًا الْوَصِيَّةُ لِلْوَالِدَيْنِ وَالْأَقْرَبِينَ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ ۖ حَقًّا عَلَى الْمُتَّقِينَ

Prescribed for you, when death approaches any of you and he leaves behind wealth, is the bequest for his parents and near relatives according to what is fair — a duty upon the righteous.

This verse establishes the bequest (wasiyyah) as the earliest framework for passing on wealth. The command was later supplemented by the detailed shares of Surah An-Nisa, but its principle remains: what one leaves behind is a trust, and disposing of it fairly is an act of taqwa.

The bequest is not a favour to the heir — it is a right owed to the family, and withholding it is described as among the sins.

2:240 · Provision for widows0.81

وَالَّذِينَ يُتَوَفَّوْنَ مِنكُمْ وَيَذَرُونَ أَزْوَاجًا وَصِيَّةً لِّأَزْوَاجِهِم مَّتَاعًا إِلَى الْحَوْلِ غَيْرَ إِخْرَاجٍ

And those of you who pass away and leave wives behind — a bequest for their wives of provision for a year without being turned out.

Here the bequest takes a specific form: securing the widow's maintenance for a full year. The verse shows inheritance law in the Qur'an begins with protection of the most vulnerable heirs before it allocates fixed shares.

4:11 · Fixed shares — children & parents0.97

يُوصِيكُمُ اللَّهُ فِي أَوْلَادِكُمْ ۖ لِلذَّكَرِ مِثْلُ حَظِّ الْأُنثَيَيْنِ ۚ فَإِن كُنَّ نِسَاءً فَوْقَ اثْنَتَيْنِ فَلَهُنَّ ثُلُثَا مَا تَرَكَ ۖ وَإِن كَانَتْ وَاحِدَةً فَلَهَا النِّصْفُ

Allah instructs you concerning your children: for the male is a share equal to that of two females. If there are only daughters, two or more, they receive two-thirds of what he leaves; if only one, she receives half.

This is the cornerstone verse of the law of shares (faraid). The opening — "Allah instructs you" — frames inheritance not as custom but as divine allocation: the estate belongs to the distribution Allah decrees, not to the wishes of the heirs. Each share named here is a ceiling set by revelation, and the commentary walks through each case in turn.

Note

The verse continues with the shares of the deceased's parents — one-sixth each where the deceased left children — completing the inner circle of heirs before more distant kin are considered.

4:12 · Fixed shares — spouses & siblings0.93

وَلَكُمْ نِصْفُ مَا تَرَكَ أَزْوَاجُكُمْ إِن لَّمْ يَكُن لَّهُنَّ وَلَدٌ ۚ فَإِن كَانَ لَهُنَّ وَلَدٌ فَلَكُمُ الرُّبُعُ مِمَّا تَرَكْنَ

And for you is half of what your wives leave if they have no child; but if they have a child, then for you is a fourth of what they leave.

The mirror verse: the husband's share of his wife's estate, then the wife's share of her husband's. The presence or absence of children changes the share — the law adjusts to the shape of the family rather than applying a flat rule. This responsiveness is repeated throughout the passage and trains the reader to see inheritance as a system, not a list.

4:176 · Al-Kalalah — those leaving no ascendant or descendant0.90

يَسْتَفْتُونَكَ قُلِ اللَّهُ يُفْتِيكُمْ فِي الْكَلَالَةِ ۚ إِنِ امْرُؤٌ هَلَكَ لَيْسَ لَهُ وَلَدٌ وَهُوَ أَخُو لِأُخْتٍ فَلَهَا نِصْفُ مَا تَرَكَ

They ask you for a ruling. Say: Allah gives you a ruling concerning al-kalalah — if a man dies having no child but has a sister, she receives half of what he left.

The final verse of the surah answers a question the community itself asked, and its placement is deliberate: the surah that opens with family law closes by completing it. Al-kalalah — one who leaves neither parent nor child — is the hardest case, and the Qur'an resolves it by revelation rather than leaving it to tribal custom.

"Allah makes clear to you the law, lest you go astray" — the closing words of the surah are its summary: inheritance is guidance, and deviating from the revealed shares is the astray-ness it warns against.
